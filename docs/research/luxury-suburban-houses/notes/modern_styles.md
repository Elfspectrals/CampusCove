# Upscale US suburban houses, five newer styles (Modern Farmhouse, luxury Craftsman, Mediterranean / Spanish Revival, Mid-Century Modern, Contemporary)

Scope note: about 16 searches and fetches. Real plan dimensions come from Architectural Designs (AD) listings. Style traits come from municipal historic-preservation guides (Glendale, Santa Barbara, Whittier, Belmont MA, PA PHMC), Houzz, an Eichler realtor glossary and design blogs. Several primary PDFs (Santa Barbara guides) could not be decoded, and Sater and The House Plan Shop returned 429/403 errors. Hex colours are approximations: SW 7005, 6258 and 7069 are Sherwin-Williams' published values, and all the other hex values are my own inference and marked as such. Imperial-to-metric conversions are mine: 1 ft = 0.3048 m; pitch x:12 converted to degrees.

## 1. Popularity and market context (all five styles)

### Takeaway
No exterior style wins nationally in NAHB's survey. Modern Farmhouse wins consumer popularity votes, while Craftsman is the most common existing stock. For a 2020s luxury estate, a mix of Farmhouse, Craftsman, Mediterranean and Contemporary is credible; Mid-Century Modern is a niche, design-led choice.

### Cited Findings
- NAHB *What Home Buyers Really Want* (2021): traditional 32%, contemporary 24%, transitional 16%, modern 14%. There was no clear national winner, and "modern farmhouse" was not one of the options. — [NAHB blog 2021](https://nahb.org/blog/2021/04/Buyers-Split-on-Exterior-Design-Preferences); [ProBuilder](https://www.probuilder.com/homebuyers-enjoy-array-exterior-styles-survey-finds)
- Homes.com vote: Modern Farmhouse was the most popular style in 42 states. Of the existing homes in the survey, 28.7% are modern farmhouses versus 30.7% "bohemian craftsman". The NYT is credited with coining "modern farmhouse" in 2016. — [Homes.com](https://www.homes.com/blog/most-popular-house-styles); [Yahoo summary](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/most-popular-home-style-u-212000516.html)
- Caveat: Homes.com is a consumer vote, not sales data, and I could not find the 2024 NAHB exterior results. — same sources

### Inferences
- In-game, the Modern Farmhouse should be the "default best-seller". Contemporary is the "top tier", Mediterranean reads as "old money / warm climate", Craftsman is "cosy premium", and MCM is the "designer collector" pick.

### Gaps
- No Zonda or 2024–25 NAHB style-share figures were found.

## 2. Lot fit and real plan dimensions (applies to all styles)

### Takeaway
Real luxury plans run about 15–26 m wide and 17–23 m deep, including an attached 3-car garage. On a 28 x 28 m lot, a footprint of about 18–22 m wide by 14–20 m deep fits with yard and pool space. One-storey luxury plans hit the upper width bound, so two storeys are the safer default.

Walls are white painted brick plus warm wood siding. The 3-car side-entry garage is 746 sq ft (~69 m²). Front porch 90 sq ft (8.4 m²), rear porch 714 sq ft (66 m²) with an outdoor kitchen and an optional outdoor fireplace. Multi-slide doors open the great room onto the porch. 3,344 heated sq ft (311 m²). — [AD 421033HFC](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/luxury-modern-farmhouse-house-plan-with-scullery-and-outdoor-kitchen-421033hfc)

  - Attached garage wing for 3 cars at about 10 x 7 m.
  - Total footprint about 20–24 m x 13–18 m.
- One-storey luxury plans like 421033HFC (25.7 m wide) use almost the whole lot width. Use them only for a "ranch estate" variant with a shallow back yard.
- Metric target areas: 3-car garage ~65–85 m², rear covered porch or loggia ~30–65 m², front porch ~8–25 m² (more for a wraparound).

## 3. Modern Farmhouse

### Takeaway
It reads as a white or light gabled box with steep roofs, black-framed windows, a deep covered front porch, board-and-batten siding and a dark standing-seam metal roof accent. This style fits low poly almost perfectly.

### Cited Findings
- Roof: steep gables and dormers. Shallow pitches make it read as a "generic ranch with farmhouse siding". — [Hackrea custom build details](https://www.hackrea.net/?p=5786379) (design blog)
- A real luxury plan uses 8:12 main and 16:12 secondary (accent gables). — [AD 421033HFC](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/luxury-modern-farmhouse-house-plan-with-scullery-and-outdoor-kitchen-421033hfc)
- Standing-seam metal goes in matte black or dark bronze. Budget builds use metal only on the porch roof and lower dormers, with architectural shingles (charcoal) on the main roof. — [Hackrea](https://www.hackrea.net/?p=5786379); [PAC-CLAD case study](https://www.pac-clad.com/?p=26519)
- Walls: white or light board-and-batten is "the most recognizable" wall treatment. It mixes with stone or brick (white-painted brick in 421033HFC) and warm wood accents. Other details are wood porch posts and beams, exposed rafters, and brackets on gables. — [Hackrea](https://www.hackrea.net/?p=5786379); [Houzz board-and-batten gallery](https://www.houzz.co.nz/photos/exterior-design-ideas-with-board-and-batten-siding-phbr2-bp~t_10808~a_88-6--3411-20901); [AD 421033HFC](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/luxury-modern-farmhouse-house-plan-with-scullery-and-outdoor-kitchen-421033hfc)
- Colours: SW Pure White 7005 on the body with SW Iron Ore 7069 trim is a documented farmhouse pairing. Tricorn Black SW 6258 was reported as the most-requested accent colour for 2025. — [Old Mission painter 2025](https://www.oldmission.net/2025/12/paint-exterior-2025-painting-by-don/); [Brick & Batten](https://www.brickandbatten.com/tricorn-black-2025/); [Color Concierge](https://thecolorconcierge.com/white-exterior-farmhouse-colors)
- Garage: a 3-car side-entry garage is the norm in the luxury plans above. — [AD plans in section 2](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/818108jss)

### Inferences
**Massing**
- Rectangle main gable (front-facing or side-facing) plus 1–2 cross-gables, making an L or T.
- Usually 1.5–2 storeys and roughly symmetrical about the entry gable.
- Garage wing set back 1–3 m, often linked by a lower "breezeway" or mudroom block.

**Roof**
- Gables at 8:12–12:12 (34–45°), with an accent front gable at up to 16:12 (53°).
- Short overhangs of 0.3–0.45 m. Crisp, thin fascia is part of the look.
- Shed-roof porch at about 3:12–4:12 in standing-seam metal.
- Optional cupola on the garage.
- Chimney is a simple rectangular box, often brick-white or black.

**Facade**
- Tall 2-over-2 or 4-pane black-framed casements, about 0.9 x 1.8 m, in regular pairs.
- A large gable window, with an optional full-height glass gable over the great room.
- Front porch 2.4–3 m deep, running 60–100% of the front width, on square posts 0.2 x 0.2 m with a header beam.
- Door is wide, often double, black or natural oak.
- Gooseneck barn lights are a cheap emissive detail.

**Palettes** (hex approximate; SW values published, the rest inferred)
1. Classic: walls #EDECE6 (Pure White), trim and windows #2F2F30 (Tricorn Black), roof #3A3A3C charcoal, porch ceiling and beams #A9764B cedar, door #2F2F30.
2. Dark: walls #434341 (Iron Ore), trim #2F2F30, roof #2B2B2B, accents #B8865B wood, door #C49A6C oak.
3. Greige: walls #D8D2C6, trim #2F2F30, roof #4A4A4A, stone base #9C958A, door #6B4A2F.
4. Soft sage (inferred): walls #B9BFAE, trim #F2F0EA, roof #333333, accents #A9764B, door #2F2F30.

**Outdoor**
- Front porch with swing and rockers; this reads at game scale.
- Rear covered porch with outdoor kitchen and fireplace; pool optional.
- Picket or horse fence is easy to build.

**Low-poly reads**
- Reads well: battens as vertical strips in a procedural texture (0.3–0.4 m spacing), black window grids, metal-seam texture, porch posts, contrasting roofs.
- Skip: brackets, corbels and exact hardware.

**Luxury vs builder grade**
- Luxury: real stone or brick base, steep roof (≥ 8:12), full metal roof or a metal and shingle mix, deep porch with wood ceiling, oversized black windows, a multi-slide wall at the back, side-load garage, and a broad massing with multiple gables.
- Builder grade: shallow 6:12 roof, vinyl B&B on the front only, front-load 2-car garage dominating the facade, a token 1.2 m porch, white vinyl windows with black grilles.

### Gaps
- No numeric pitch from an authoritative style guide; the only numbers are from AD 421033HFC.

## 4. Craftsman (luxury)

### Takeaway
Low-to-moderate gables with wide open eaves, exposed rafter tails, knee braces and heavy tapered porch columns on stone piers, in earthy natural materials. The luxury version is bigger and multi-gabled, with full-height stone piers and a stained-wood garage door.

### Cited Findings
- Low or moderate front-gable roofs (hips also allowed) with wide overhanging eaves. Rafter ends are exposed instead of being boxed in a soffit, and triangular knee braces sit under the gables. Short, wide tapered porch posts stand on massive stone or brick piers that run to the ground. False beams sit under the gables, and the materials are wood, stone and brick. — [City of Whittier](https://cityofwhittier.org/government/community-development/planning-services/historic-preservation/common-residential-architectural-styles-in-whittier-to-1950/craftsman-arts-and-crafts-1905-1930); [Houzz Roots of Style](https://www.houzz.com/magazine/roots-of-style-see-what-defines-a-craftsman-home-stsetivw-vs~14999574); [Belmont MA](https://belmont-ma.gov/1681/Craftsman-Bungalow-1910-to-1930); [Glendale guide](https://glendaleca.gov/home/showdocument?id=12690)
- Modern plans keep tapered columns on stacked-stone or stone-veneer bases, with 3-car side-entry garages. — [AD 18303BE](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/18303BE); [AD 72701DA](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/craftsman-house-plan-with-3-car-garage-3-beds-and-open-living-2662-sq-ft-72701da)
- Craftsman door: the typical design has a glass top row of small lites over vertical panels, sometimes with a dentil shelf. — [Door n More](https://www.doornmore.com/blog/craftsman-home-door); [Frank Betz details](https://frankbetzhouseplans.com/house-plan-news/designing-a-craftsman-style-home-its-all-in-the-details/)

### Inferences
**Massing**
- 1.5–2 storeys with a main gable plus a front-gabled porch projection. Asymmetric is typical.
- Luxury version: L or T footprint, 16–20 m wide.

**Roof**
- 5:12–8:12 (23–34°) gables. Overhangs are wide at 0.6–0.9 m, with visible rafter tails done as a sawtooth strip of small boxes under the eave.
- Architectural shingle in brown or charcoal.
- Stone chimney on the side wall, tapered or stepped.
- Shed dormers are common.

**Facade**
- Mixed cladding: stone wainscot to ~1 m, horizontal lap siding on the ground floor, shingle or B&B in the gables.
- Wide trim of 0.15–0.2 m around windows, with a thicker head trim.
- Windows are 3-over-1 or 4-over-1 (vertical muntins in the top sash only), ganged in 2–3s.
- Porch 2.4–3 m deep with tapered columns. Low-poly version: a frustum box, 0.3 m wide at the bottom and 0.2 m at the top, on a 0.6 x 0.6 x 1.0 m stone pier.

**Palettes** (inferred hex)
1. Earth: walls #6E6B4F olive, trim #E8E0CC cream, roof #4A3F35 brown, stone #8C7A66, door #7A4A26 stained.
2. Slate: walls #5F6B70 blue-grey, trim #F0EDE4, roof #3A3A3C, stone #9A9288, door #8A5A2E.
3. Bungalow red: walls #8A4B3A, trim #D9CDB0, roof #3B3530, stone #7F7468, door #5A3A22.
4. Modern Craftsman: walls #EDECE6, trim #2F2F30, roof #3A3A3C, stone #A49C90, door #9C6A3C cedar.

**Garage**
- Side-load on luxury builds.
- A front-load garage is acceptable if it gets carriage-style doors with a stained-wood look, a small gable with brackets above, and is set back.

**Outdoor**
- Deep front porch with a low stone wall, a pergola on the side, and a rear covered patio with stone fireplace.

**Low-poly reads**
- Reads well: tapered column-on-pier (the signature), wide eaves, rafter-tail strip, knee-brace triangles (two thin boxes or a triangular prism per gable), stone base texture, lap/shingle texture switch.
- Skip: dentil shelves, art-glass door lites, exact bracket profiles.

**Luxury vs builder grade**
- Luxury: real stone piers to the ground, cedar shake gables, multiple porch gables, a stained-wood door and garage, thick trims.
- Builder grade: one column on a short half-height pier, vinyl, a narrow eave, a front garage dominating the facade.

### Gaps
- Santa Barbara Craftsman PDF not decodable, so no authoritative numeric eave depth or pitch.

## 5. Mediterranean / Spanish Revival

### Takeaway
Smooth white or cream stucco, low-pitched red half-round clay tile roofs, round arches, an asymmetric plan, and courtyard or loggia living around a pool. The luxury suburban version is a Sater-style courtyard home.

### Cited Findings
- Roof pitch is low, "usually 4:12 or less" (≤ 18.4°), and an asymmetric plan is the norm. A flat-roof-with-parapet variant is related to Pueblo Revival. — [Santa Barbara SCR guidelines (via search snippet; PDF not decodable)](https://santabarbaraca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Services/Historic%20Resources%20Design%20Guidelines/Historic_Design_Guidelines_Spanish%20Colonial%20Revival.pdf)
- Half-round clay tiles are the most obvious hallmark; chimneys are capped with tile too. Stucco is smooth, white or cream, or rough to imitate adobe. Round arches appear over the entry, porch and windows, with porch arcades on columns. — [Houzz 9 elements](https://www.houzz.in/magazine/9-architectural-elements-of-the-spanish-revival-style-stsetivw-vs~104525774); [PA PHMC](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/spanish-colonial-revival.html)
- Glendale describes wide eaves that are typically boxed, with decorative brackets. That conflicts with the common minimal-eave look, so it varies by sub-type. — [Glendale guide](https://glendaleca.gov/home/showdocument?id=12892)
- Cheap tract versions hid a flat roof behind a false strip of tile. — [Houzz 9 elements](https://www.houzz.in/magazine/9-architectural-elements-of-the-spanish-revival-style-stsetivw-vs~104525774)
- Luxury courtyard plans put the pool in a walled courtyard, with a loggia or "solana", outdoor kitchen and fireplace, and sometimes a guesthouse. — [Sater Casoria](https://saterdesign.com/products/casoria); [Sater Ferretti](https://saterdesign.com/product/ferretti/); [Sater Salcito](https://saterdesign.com/products/salcito)
- Arched entries are a signature: Sater's "Arch of an Idea" features four memorable arched entrances. — [Builder](https://builderonline.com/design/plans/the-arch-of-an-idea-4-memorable-entrances-from-dan-sater_o)

### Inferences
**Massing**
- 1–2 storeys in an L, U or courtyard form. Asymmetric, stepped volumes of different heights: a 2-storey tower block plus 1-storey wings.
- Courtyard about 8–12 m square, enclosed by the house on three sides and a 2 m wall on the fourth.

**Roof**
- 3:12–4:12 (14–18°) gables and hips. Overhang is either minimal (0.15–0.3 m, Spanish) or 0.6 m boxed (Mediterranean/Tuscan).
- Use a corrugated half-round tile texture; real tile geometry is not needed.
- Chimney is a stucco box with a small tile hip cap.

**Facade**
- Fewer, smaller and deeply recessed windows. Reveal 0.2–0.3 m; insetting the window plane is cheap and it sells "thick walls".
- One large arched opening is the focal point: arched entry, picture window or arcade. Approximate the arch with an n-segment polygon (6–8 segments) or a flat rectangle with an arch cut-out in a texture.
- Wrought-iron balconettes and window grilles go on thin box rails.
- Wood door, often arched, in dark stain.

**Palettes** (inferred hex)
1. Classic Spanish: walls #F2EBDD, roof #B5532F terracotta, trim and ironwork #1F1F1F, door #5A3A22, tile accent #2E5E8C blue.
2. Tuscan: walls #D9B98C ochre, roof #A0522D, trim #6B4A2F, stone #B8A27E, door #4A2F1E.
3. Santa Barbara white: walls #FAF7F0, roof #C0643A, trim #2B2B2B, shutters #3C5A3C green, door #6B4226.
4. Modern Mediterranean: walls #E9E4DA, roof #8C4A32 (darker tile) or flat, trim #2F2F30 steel windows, accent #A9764B oak.

**Garage**
- Courtyard-entry or side-load so doors never face the street.
- Or a separate garage wing with an arched or wood-look door.

**Outdoor**
- Loggia (an arcade of 3–5 arches), walled courtyard with fountain, pool, pergola, outdoor kitchen and fireplace, and a roof terrace on the flat-roof variant.
- At game scale, the fountain, arcade, pool and cypress or palm trees read very well.

**Low-poly reads**
- Reads well: terracotta roof colour, white walls, deep reveals, one arcade, iron rails, a tower volume.
- Skip: carved corbels, decorative tile insets (use a flat colour patch), Moorish ornament, rafter details.

**Luxury vs builder grade**
- Luxury: real courtyard, varied rooflines and a tower, deep reveals, real barrel tile (rounded, irregular), stone surrounds at the entry, a loggia with pool.
- Builder grade: flat concrete "S" tile in a uniform colour, tan stucco box, front garage, faux arch only over the door.

### Gaps
- Sater width and depth not retrieved (429), and no numeric eave depth from an authoritative source.

## 6. Mid-Century Modern (new-build MCM)

### Takeaway
Long, low, horizontal one-storey volumes under flat, low-pitched, butterfly or shed roofs with deep eaves. A closed street face with clerestories and a carport opens to full glass walls at the back. Wood, brick and stone are the materials.

### Cited Findings
- Roofs are flat (a slight, invisible drainage slope), low-pitched, butterfly (two panels sloping to a V valley) or folded-plate. — [Eichler Homes glossary](http://www.eichlerhomesforsale.com/eichler-homes-blog/mcm-university-a-beginners-guide-to-mid-century-design-terms-every-buyer-should-know)
- Post-and-beam structure allows large glass areas. Mostly one storey, long and low, close to the ground. Cantilevered carport roofs and balconies. — [Eichler glossary](http://www.eichlerhomesforsale.com/eichler-homes-blog/mcm-university-a-beginners-guide-to-mid-century-design-terms-every-buyer-should-know)
- Openings and outdoor spaces:
  - Central open-air atrium, plus entry atriums.
  - Clerestory windows just below the roofline for light with privacy.
  - Floor-to-ceiling glass walls and sliders to the back.
  - Corner windows, picture windows.
  - Breeze-block screens.
  - Source: [Eichler glossary](http://www.eichlerhomesforsale.com/eichler-homes-blog/mcm-university-a-beginners-guide-to-mid-century-design-terms-every-buyer-should-know)
- Street front: modest Eichler facades with a carport and frosted privacy windows, opening to glass and courtyards at the back. Carports are open roof-and-post shelters, often integrated into the main roof. — [Eichler glossary](http://www.eichlerhomesforsale.com/eichler-homes-blog/mcm-university-a-beginners-guide-to-mid-century-design-terms-every-buyer-should-know)
- Materials: vertical board-and-batten, clapboard in earth tones, wood with stone or brick, and porthole windows in doors or garage doors. — [Eichler glossary](http://www.eichlerhomesforsale.com/eichler-homes-blog/mcm-university-a-beginners-guide-to-mid-century-design-terms-every-buyer-should-know); [Midcentury Modern Gal](https://midcenturymoderngal.com/blogs/news/10-popular-exterior-features-of-mid-century-modern-homes)
- Strong horizontal emphasis, minimal ornament, deep eaves, vertical cedar and brick. — [A2modern](https://a2modern.org/?p=28644); [Hobbs Modern](https://hobbsmodern.com/a/blog/mid-century-modern-architecture-homes-a-buyers-guide)
- AD sells a new-build "mid-century modern" plan (81832AB, 3,459 sq ft, 40' x 93'6" = 12.2 x 28.5 m) with an optional third-level roof deck, so the label is stretched in modern catalogues. — [AD 81832AB](https://api.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/mid-century-modern-home-plan-with-optional-lower-level-and-3rd-level-rooftop-deck-3459-sq-ft-81832ab)

### Inferences
**Massing**
- One storey, about 3.0–3.6 m to the eave. Rectangle, L or U around an atrium. Footprint ~22 x 14 m on the lot.
- Split volumes: bedroom wing and living wing joined by a glass entry.

**Roof**
- Flat with a thin, deep fascia (0.25–0.35 m) or a 1:12–3:12 (5–14°) low gable.
- Butterfly: two sheds at about 2:12 meeting in a centre valley.
- Deep eaves of 0.9–1.5 m. This is the key horizontal shadow line.
- Gravel or white membrane texture. Chimney is a wide, low brick or stone mass.

**Facade**
- Street side: mostly solid wall, a clerestory ribbon (0.4–0.6 m tall under the eave), and a carport.
- Back side: a full-height glass wall from post to post every 2.4–3.6 m, in thin dark or natural aluminium frames.
- Entry is a bold coloured single door (often off-centre) with sidelights or a vertical panel pattern.

**Palettes** (inferred hex)
1. Eichler: walls #8A6A4A stained vertical wood, trim and fascia #F2EFE8, roof #D8D8D8 membrane, door #E07A1F orange, accent #3C6E71 teal.
2. Palm Springs: walls #F4F1EA, trim #2A2A2A, roof #EDEDED, breeze block #FFFFFF, door #E8B923 yellow.
3. Brick ranch: walls #9C5B3E brick, siding #6B4A2F, fascia #2F2F30, roof #555555, door #2E7D6B green.
4. Modern MCM: walls #2F2F30 charred wood look, accents #B8865B cedar, roof #3A3A3C, door #C4562A rust.

**Garage**
- Carport under an extension of the main roof, often cantilevered. Cheapest to build and very on-style.
- Or a flush, slab-door garage with no panels.

**Outdoor**
- Atrium courtyard, a rear patio under the deep eave, a kidney or rectangle pool (period-correct), a breeze-block screen wall, low desert plantings.

**Low-poly reads**
- Reads well: thin flat slab roof with deep overhang, butterfly roof, clerestory strip, glass wall, carport posts, breeze-block grid texture, a bright door. All are boxes, so this is the easiest style.
- Skip: folded-plate roofs (optional), jalousies, porthole details.

**Luxury vs builder grade**
- Luxury: deep cantilevered eaves, real floor-to-ceiling glass, an atrium, natural wood plus stone or brick, a pool, careful horizontal alignment of all lines.
- Builder grade: a flat-roof box with shallow eaves, small windows, vinyl.

### Gaps
- Few US production builders sell new MCM in suburbs, and no popularity data was found. No primary source gave eave dimensions.

## 7. Contemporary / Modern (flat or shed roofs, large glazing)

### Takeaway
Interlocking or cantilevered rectilinear volumes with flat roofs behind parapets, or single-slope sheds. Large floor-to-ceiling glazing in slim black frames, and mixed cladding of render or stucco, dark metal panels and warm vertical wood. Often a roof deck. Garages are flush and modern (glass or slab doors).

### Cited Findings
- Roof is flat with a parapet, a single-slope shed or low-pitch standing seam. Windows are large fixed glazing, often floor-to-ceiling, in slim black or charcoal frames. Typical scheme: "dark grey render + black metal panels + vertical wood entry accent + flat roof + ribbon windows". — [Genroom contemporary facade guide](https://genroom.io/design/contemporary-facade) (design/AI site, low authority)
- Houzz galleries show many flat-roof, mixed-cladding examples (white or neutral render, wood, metal). — [Houzz contemporary mixed cladding](https://www.houzz.co.uk/photos/contemporary-house-exterior-with-mixed-cladding-ideas-and-designs-phbr2-bp~t_10378~s_2203~a_32-504?pg=2); [Houzz flat roof](https://www.houzz.co.uk/photos/contemporary-house-exterior-with-a-flat-roof-ideas-and-designs-phbr2-bp~t_10378~s_2203~a_69-550?pg=11)
- Large windows are favoured over swathes of bi-folds (UK source, 2025). — [Ideal Home](https://www.idealhome.co.uk/renovation/home-renovation-trends)
- US contemporary plans often include roof decks of 374–510 sq ft (35–47 m²), and heights are 6.4 m (one storey, flat) to 10.9 m (two storeys plus deck). — [AD 666258RAF](https://app.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/contemporary-house-plan-with-two-flex-rooms-a-rooftop-deck-3510-sq-ft-666258raf); [AD 666246RAF](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/modern-house-plan-with-rooftop-deck-for-a-front-sloping-lot-4972-sq-ft-666246raf); [AD 570006LEF](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/3-bed-modern-home-plan-with-flat-roof-design-under-2500-square-feet-570006lef); [AD 81683AB](https://app.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/modern-house-plan-with-roof-top-deck-81683ab)
- "Contemporary" was the #2 buyer preference (24%) and "modern" 14% in NAHB 2021. — [NAHB](https://nahb.org/blog/2021/04/Buyers-Split-on-Exterior-Design-Preferences)

### Inferences
**Massing**
- Two storeys, 3.3–3.6 m floor-to-floor.
- Two or three stacked or offset boxes: a wide ground box plus an upper box shifted 1.5–3 m to cantilever over the entry or patio.
- Asymmetric. Footprint ~20 x 15 m.

**Roof**
- Flat with a 0.3–0.6 m parapet (a thin cap trim in metal), or a mono-pitch shed at 2:12–4:12.
- Clerestory band where two sheds meet at different heights.
- Hidden or minimal overhang, except a deliberate thin canopy (0.15–0.25 m slab) over the entry or windows.
- Roof deck with a glass balustrade and a stair or elevator box.

**Facade**
- Large glazing: floor-to-ceiling panels 1.2–3 m wide in black frames, a corner glass wrap, a 2-storey glass stair tower.
- Ribbon windows (0.6 m tall horizontal strips).
- Cladding blocks map to volumes: one volume in white or grey render, one in dark metal or fibre-cement panels, one in vertical wood slats. Material changes happen at volume edges, never mid-wall.
- Oversized pivot door, 1.2–1.5 m wide x 2.7–3 m tall, in wood or black, with a full-height sidelight and an inset entry.

**Palettes** (hex approximate)
1. White modern: walls #F2F2EE render, frames #2F2F30 (Tricorn Black), cladding #B8865B cedar slats, parapet cap #3A3A3C, door #8A5A2E.
2. Dark: walls #3E4042 render, panels #1F1F21, wood #C49A6C, frames #1A1A1A, door #C49A6C.
3. Warm minimal: walls #D9D2C5, stone #B5AC9E limestone, frames #4A4A48 bronze, wood #A9764B, door #2F2F30.
4. Desert modern (inferred): walls #CDBBA3 sand, panels #6D4C3D corten-like #8B4A2B, frames #2F2F30, door #8B4A2B.

**Garage**
- Front-facing but flush with the facade, in its own clad volume.
- Flush slab doors in wood slats or aluminium and frosted glass, or a courtyard-entry layout.
- An auto court with paving is a strong luxury cue.

**Outdoor**
- Infinity-edge or rectangular pool aligned with the glass wall, a large concrete pad patio under a cantilever.
- Roof terrace with lounge furniture, a linear fire pit, horizontal slat fence.
- At game scale, the roof deck and pool read very well.

**Low-poly reads**
- Reads well: the box game itself (cantilevers, offsets), parapets, glass walls with emissive interiors at night, slat textures, LED soffit strips, a pivot door.
- Skip: glass balustrade fittings (use a single transparent plane), complex fins.

**Luxury vs builder grade**
- Luxury: true cantilevers, oversized glazing with thin frames, real material blocking (wood, stone, metal), a hidden gutter or flat roof, a pivot door, a roof deck, pool, landscape lighting.
- Builder grade: a regular box with standard-size windows, a single render colour, a shallow "fake modern" shed roof on a traditional plan, a raised-panel garage door.

### Gaps
- No authoritative US 2024–25 luxury contemporary trend report (Zonda, AD magazine) was retrieved, so the materials and colour claims rest on lower-authority design sites plus my inference.

## 8. Cross-style notes for the generator

### Takeaway
All five styles can be built from about 10 parameterised pieces: wall box, gable roof, hip roof, shed roof, flat roof with parapet, porch (posts plus roof), window (frame plus grid plus glass), door, trim strip and chimney box. They are separated by roof pitch and overhang, the cladding texture set, window grid type, porch or loggia variant and palette.

### Cited Findings
- Pitch and overhang ranges come from sections 3–7: Spanish ≤ 4:12 ([Santa Barbara](https://santabarbaraca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Services/Historic%20Resources%20Design%20Guidelines/Historic_Design_Guidelines_Spanish%20Colonial%20Revival.pdf)), Farmhouse 8:12 / 16:12 ([AD 421033HFC](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/luxury-modern-farmhouse-house-plan-with-scullery-and-outdoor-kitchen-421033hfc)), MCM flat or low ([Eichler glossary](http://www.eichlerhomesforsale.com/eichler-homes-blog/mcm-university-a-beginners-guide-to-mid-century-design-terms-every-buyer-should-know)).

### Inferences
Style parameter table (my synthesis):

| Style | Storeys | Roof | Pitch | Overhang | Garage | Signature piece |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modern Farmhouse | 1.5–2 | gable + metal shed porch | 34–53° | 0.3–0.45 m | side-load 3-car | deep front porch + B&B |
| Craftsman luxe | 1.5–2 | gable, rafter tails | 23–34° | 0.6–0.9 m | side-load | tapered column on stone pier |
| Spanish/Med | 1–2, tower | low gable/hip clay | 14–18° | 0.15–0.6 m | courtyard/side | arcade loggia + courtyard |
| Mid-Century | 1 | flat / butterfly / low gable | 0–14° | 0.9–1.5 m | carport | deep flat eave + glass wall |
| Contemporary | 2 | flat parapet / shed | 0–18° | ~0 (canopy) | flush front or auto court | cantilevered box + roof deck |

- Universal price cues: side-load or courtyard garage (no doors facing the street), larger glazing, a mix of 2–3 real materials, roof complexity (several gables or volumes), a deep outdoor room at the back, a pool, landscape and soffit lighting, and a wide oversized front door.
