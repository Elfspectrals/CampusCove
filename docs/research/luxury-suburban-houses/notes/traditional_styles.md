# Upscale US suburban houses in five traditional styles: exterior design for a low-poly procedural generator

Styles covered: Colonial Revival (CR), Georgian / Georgian Revival (GE), Cape Cod upscale/expanded (CC), Tudor Revival (TU), Victorian Queen Anne / Folk Victorian (QA).
Unit conversion: 1 ft = 0.3048 m. Roof pitch: 8:12 = 33.7 deg, 10:12 = 39.8 deg, 12:12 = 45 deg.
Labels: anything under "Cited Findings" comes from the linked source. Numbers under "Inferences" (hex colours, trim sizes, metre rules of thumb) are my own generator-oriented derivations and are not quoted from a source.

## 1. Massing: storeys, footprint, symmetry, luxury dimensions (lot ~28 x 28 m)

### Takeaway
GE and CR are symmetrical two-storey boxes with a five-bay front, usually with a garage wing or ell set behind them. CC is a 1.5-storey symmetrical box that luxury plans grow into a main block plus wings or ells about 19-24 m wide. TU and QA are asymmetrical compositions of several gabled volumes; QA adds a corner tower. Most published luxury plans run 18-25 m wide by 15-25 m deep, so a 28 m lot holds one only if the garage sits in the depth (side-load) and not across the front.

### Cited Findings
- GE: symmetrical, typically two storeys; front usually five bays with a centre door; smaller examples are three bays wide ("two-thirds Georgian", side door) — [PHMC Georgian](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/georgian.html)
- GE luxury plan 750032LDG: 58'0" W x 65'4" D (17.7 x 19.9 m), two storeys, 3,525 sq ft (327 m2) split almost evenly over the two floors, max ridge 32' (9.75 m), first-floor ceilings 9'1" (2.77 m), second floor 8'1" (2.46 m), two-storey foyer 18' (5.5 m), three-car attached side-entry garage of 690 sq ft (64 m2) — [Architectural Designs 750032LDG](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/elegant-georgian-house-plan-featuring-five-bedrooms-and-three-car-garage-750032ldg)
- GE plan 72661DA: 75'10" x 79'7" (23.1 x 24.3 m), three-car side-entry garage, brick front; plan 196-1273: 99'10" x 129'8" (30.4 x 39.5 m), 7,200 sq ft, too big for a 28 m lot — search-result snippets from [Architectural Designs 72661DA](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/stately-4-5-bed-georgian-house-plan-with-hexagonal-great-room-72661da) and [ThePlanCollection 32490](https://theplancollection.com/house-plans/plan-7200-square-feet-5-bedroom-5-5-bathroom-georgian-style-32490) (pages not opened; check before relying on them)
- CR: symmetry is central; simple forms, accentuated entry, restrained classical detail — [Wikipedia, Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Revival_architecture); [Florence AL style guide (search summary)](https://florenceal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Colonial-Revival-Style-Guide.pdf)
- CR houses are "usually larger than their prototypes" and "often exaggerate architectural details" (Cyril M. Harris); the style peaked 1910-1930, when it accounted for about 40% of US homes; today's "New Traditional" homes draw on it — [Wikipedia, Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Revival_architecture)
- CC: generally 1.5 storeys, small and boxy, symmetrical facade of three or five bays — [PHMC Cape Cod field guide](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/pa-suburbs/field-guide/cape-cod.html)
- Expanded CC plans (search snippets): Shamrock Point 73' x 71'5" (22.3 x 21.8 m), three-car side-entry garage; Edison Hill 76'10" x 50' (23.4 x 15.2 m), two-car side-entry; Larkspur 80'8" x 51'8" (24.6 x 15.7 m), three-car garage off to the side; Plan 174-1075 62' x 82' (18.9 x 25.0 m) with breezeway to a detached garage; Shingle 703 117' x 55' (35.7 x 16.8 m) — [Houseplansandmore 036D-0120](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan036D-0120.aspx); [Houseplansandmore 055D-0803](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan055D-0803.aspx); [TheHouseDesigners Larkspur](https://thehousedesigners.com/plan/larkspur-7872); [ThePlanCollection 11291](https://theplancollection.com/house-plans/home-plan-11291); [TheHouseDesigners 6474](https://www.thehousedesigners.com/plan/6474)
- TU: picturesque, medieval-cottage massing with overhanging gables or second storeys; facades dominated by one or more steep cross gables — [PHMC Tudor Revival](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/tudor-revival.html); [Dallas Tudor guide (search summary)](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/pnv/PublishingImages/Pages/Guides/Tudor_Style%20Details.pdf)
- TU luxury plans (search snippets): Rolling Green 68' x 64'2" (20.7 x 19.6 m), 3,773 sq ft, three-car side-entry; Glen Hollow 80'10" x 72'6" (24.6 x 22.1 m), 4,185 sq ft, three-car side-entry; 9369EL 71'10" x 93'4" (21.9 x 28.4 m); 195-1337 90' x 102' (27.4 x 31.1 m), ridge 32' (9.75 m), brick and stucco — [Houseplansandmore 031D-0037](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan031D-0037.aspx); [Houseplansandmore 031D-0036](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan031D-0036.aspx); [Architectural Designs 9369EL](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/a-touch-of-tudor-9369el); [ThePlanCollection 33577](https://theplancollection.com/house-plans/plan-6179-square-feet-4-bedroom-5-5-bathroom-tudor-style-33577)

Listings do not give turret sizes — [TheHousePlanShop 047H-0006](https://www.thehouseplanshop.com/plans/plan-047h-0006); [TheHousePlanShop 032H-0044](https://www.thehouseplanshop.com/plans/plan-032h-0044); [TheHousePlanShop 043H-0136](https://www.thehouseplanshop.com/plans/plan-043h-0136)

### Inferences
- Generator templates (metres, main body without garage):
  - GE: one rectangle 16-19 W x 10-12 D, 2 storeys at about 3.0 m floor-to-floor, plus optional symmetrical 1-1.5-storey flanking wings 5-6 m wide, set back 1-2 m. Strict mirror symmetry.
  - CR: rectangle 14-17 W x 9-11 D, 2 storeys, plus one side wing or rear ell (garage or sunroom), which may break overall symmetry while the main block stays symmetrical.
  - CC upscale: a 1.5-storey main block 12-14 W x 8-9 D (eaves about 1 m above the second-floor level), then a lower connector or breezeway wing, then a garage wing, laid out in a line or an L. This "big house, little house, back house, barn" stepping is how upscale capes add size. The stepping is my inference from the breezeway and side-garage plans above; I did not find a source for it.
  - TU: L or T footprint, 13-18 W x 11-15 D, 2 storeys, with 1-2 front-facing gabled bays projecting 1.5-3 m and a one-storey entry vestibule gable in front of one of them.
  - QA: rectangle 12-15 W x 12-16 D plus a front cross-gable bay projecting 1.5-2.5 m on one side, a round or polygonal corner tower 3-4 m across on the opposite corner, and a porch 2.4-3 m deep wrapping the front and one side.
- On a 28 m lot with side setbacks of roughly 3 m, the usable width is about 22 m. That fits all the plan widths above except the largest, provided the garage is side-loaded and placed toward the rear.

### Gaps
- No source gives typical tower diameters or porch depths in feet; the values above are estimates.
- Most plan dimensions come from search snippets. Only 750032LDG was opened and verified.

## 2. Roof: form, pitch, dormers, eaves, chimneys

### Takeaway
GE: hip or side gable at moderate pitch, with end or paired chimneys. CR: side gable (later examples) or hip (earlier), plus pedimented dormers. CC: steep side gable of 8:12-12:12 with two or three gabled dormers and a large chimney. TU: very steep front and cross gables with a massive decorated chimney on the facade. QA: steep, irregular roof with cross gables, a conical or polygonal tower roof, and tall chimneys.

### Cited Findings
- GE: side-gabled or hipped roof; dentiled cornice — [PHMC Georgian](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/georgian.html)
- A modern GE luxury plan uses a 12:12 primary pitch, with multiple gables and dormers and arched windows — [Architectural Designs 750032LDG](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/elegant-georgian-house-plan-featuring-five-bedrooms-and-three-car-garage-750032ldg)
- CR: gabled, hipped or gambrel roofs; broken pediments are common — [Wikipedia, Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Revival_architecture). Early examples are more often hipped, later ones predominantly gabled — [City of Anaheim, American Colonial Revival (search summary)](https://www.anaheim.net/DocumentCenter/View/32598/American-Colonial-Revival)
- CC: steep side-gabled roof with end gables; dormers common; side chimney — [PHMC Cape Cod](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/pa-suburbs/field-guide/cape-cod.html). Pitch is typically 8:12 to 12:12 (about 34-45 deg) — [Family Home Plans blog (search summary)](https://blog.familyhomeplans.com/?p=3061). Sources disagree on chimney placement (traditional large centre chimney vs side or exterior-wall chimney in revivals) and on whether dormers are original or added in the 1920s — [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/blog/what-is-a-cape-cod-house-style/); [Wikipedia, Cape Cod house](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod_house)
- TU: steeply pitched roof, often with front-facing or multiple gables and cross gables; some roofs curve over the eaves to imitate thatch; parapeted or Flemish gables appear; prominent decorative chimneys on the front or side — [PHMC Tudor Revival](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/tudor-revival.html). The roof is sometimes hipped — [Whittier Tudor Revival (search summary)](https://www.cityofwhittier.org/government/community-development/planning-services/historic-preservation/common-residential-architectural-styles-in-whittier-to-1950/tudor-revival-1890-1940)
- QA: steep, irregularly shaped roof with cross gables or large dormers; round or polygonal corner tower with a conical roof — [PHMC Queen Anne](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/queen-anne.html). Not every QA has a tower; some use a cutaway bay with a decorated gable instead — [Indiana PA Queen Anne walk (search summary)](https://icopd.org/assets/queen-anne-walk_2021-pdf.pdf)
- CR trim: cornice returns at the gable ends reduce the big triangular field of siding and "bring the roofline back to the house" — [Fine Homebuilding](https://finehomebuilding.com/2016/05/16/upgrading-colonial-exteriors)

### Inferences
- Generator parameters (pitch, eave overhang, chimneys):
  - GE: hip at 6:12-8:12 (27-34 deg) or side gable at 8:12-10:12. Shallow eave of 0.3-0.4 m, boxed with a thick cornice band. Two brick end chimneys, or four paired chimneys on a hip roof, each about 0.6 x 1.2 m, rising 1 m above the ridge.
  - CR: side gable at 8:12-10:12; overhang 0.3 m with gable returns; 3 pedimented dormers on the front slope; 1 exterior end chimney in brick.
  - CC: side gable at 10:12-12:12; tight eaves of 0.15-0.25 m (a defining look: the roof hugs the walls); 2-3 gabled dormers, or one shed dormer at the rear; 1 large chimney in the centre of the ridge or at the end. Wings use the same pitch with lower ridges.
  - TU: main and cross gables at 12:12-18:12 (45-56 deg), with uneven ridge heights; overhang 0.2-0.4 m with thick bargeboards; one massive chimney on the front facade, 1.0-1.5 m wide, tapering through 1-2 set-backs and capped by 2-3 separate flue pots.
  - QA: main hip, or a front gable at 10:12-12:12, plus 1-3 cross gables; overhang 0.4-0.6 m; tower roof is a cone (or an 8-sided pyramid in low poly) at 1.5-2 times the tower radius in height, rising above the main eave; 2 tall, slim brick chimneys.
- Gambrel (Dutch Colonial Revival) is a CR variant and worth one generator option.

### Gaps
- I found no source quantifying eave overhangs or chimney dimensions per style.

## 3. Facade composition: windows, shutters, entrance, porches

### Takeaway
GE and CR: a strict 5-bay grid of 6-over-6 double-hung windows aligned between floors, a centre door with a pediment or portico, fanlight and sidelights, and properly sized shutters. CC: 3 or 5 bays on the ground floor and dormers above that line up with them. TU: irregular groups of narrow casements, round-arched plank door in a gabled entry, often no shutters. QA: bays, an oriel or tower windows, large single-pane sash, and a deep wraparound porch with turned posts.

### Cited Findings
- GE: multi-pane sash, commonly 6-over-6 (6 to 20 panes per sash); five bays with centre door; panelled door with transom and a pediment or crown with flanking pilasters — [PHMC Georgian](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/georgian.html)
- CR: double-hung sash often with shutters; fanlights and sidelights; centre entry with columns or extended portico — [Wikipedia, Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Revival_architecture); [Lancaster PA style page (search summary)](https://cityoflancasterpa.com/colonial-revival-style-0)
- Well-detailed colonial facades: divided-lite double-hung sash; entablature (architrave, frieze, cornice) linking the second-floor windows to the roofline and a similar entablature over the entry; sidelites and robust pilasters; a front porch — [Fine Homebuilding](https://finehomebuilding.com/2016/05/16/upgrading-colonial-exteriors)
- Shutters must look able to close: each is half the window width and full height, arched to match arched windows, hung on hinges from the casing, never fixed flat to siding. No shutters on garages or on openings that cannot be correctly fitted; "Better to have nothing" than wrong shutters — [Old House Guy](https://www.oldhouseguy.com/shutters-good-vs-bad-examples/)
- CC: symmetrical facade, centred door flanked by windows, three or five bays — [PHMC Cape Cod](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/pa-suburbs/field-guide/cape-cod.html)
- TU: narrow multi-pane windows including diamond-pane casements; entry porches or gabled entries; round-arched board-and-batten front doors — [PHMC Tudor Revival](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/tudor-revival.html)
- QA: projecting bay windows; single-pane sash, some with small decorative panes or stained glass; large partial or full-width porch with turned posts or columns and spindlework — [PHMC Queen Anne](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/queen-anne.html). Porches were often L-shaped, wrapping the front and sometimes the sides — [Wikipedia, Queen Anne US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Anne_style_architecture_in_the_United_States)

### Inferences
- Window and entrance parameters:
  - GE and CR windows: about 0.9 W x 1.6-1.8 H on the ground floor, slightly shorter (1.4-1.5 H) upstairs; 5 bays evenly spaced (about 2.8-3.2 m centres); a mullion grid texture of 3 x 2 lights per sash (6-over-6).
  - Door: 1.0 x 2.3 m, with sidelights of 0.35 m each side and a half-round fanlight or flat transom above. Entrance options, by cost tier: (a) flat pediment on pilasters; (b) one-storey portico 3-4 m wide x 1.8-2.4 m deep with 2-4 columns and a triangular pediment; (c) GE grand option: a full-height two-storey portico (four columns, about 6.5 m tall).
  - Palladian window: a three-part window (centre arched, narrower flanking lights) centred over the door on the second floor of GE/CR. In low poly it is three boxes with a half-disc texture on the centre one.
- CC: ground floor 2 windows either side of the door (5-bay) or 1 either side (3-bay); dormers centred over the ground-floor openings; a shallow gable or arched hood over the door, no deep porch; shutters on every main window.
- TU: windows in groups of 2-4 narrow casements (0.5 W x 1.4 H each) with a stone or wood lintel; one tall stair window in the main gable; no shutters; the door is recessed in a small steep gable or stone arch.
- QA: tall, narrow 1-over-1 windows (0.8 x 1.9 m); a 3-sided bay on the front gable; tower windows on every face; porch 2.4-3 m deep along the front and one side, posts every 2-2.5 m, a balustrade 0.9 m high, and a small pediment over the steps.

### Gaps
- Window dimensions are not given by the sources; the values above are typical-practice estimates.

## 4. Materials and palettes (with hex approximations)

### Takeaway
GE: red brick with white trim and a dark slate roof. CR: white or pale clapboard (or brick) with black or green shutters. CC: white clapboard or grey weathered shingles. TU: brick or stone ground floor, with cream stucco and dark half-timbering above. QA: multicolour paint, with different body colours on lower clapboards and upper fish-scale shingles plus contrasting trim and accents.

### Cited Findings
- GE walls: stone or brick, water table or belt course, quoins at corners — [PHMC Georgian](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/georgian.html)
- Modern GE luxury plan exterior: brick and siding — [Architectural Designs 750032LDG](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/elegant-georgian-house-plan-featuring-five-bedrooms-and-three-car-garage-750032ldg)
- TU: stucco, masonry or masonry veneer, patterned stonework or brickwork; decorative half-timbering on some wall surfaces — [PHMC Tudor Revival](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/tudor-revival.html). Half-timbering is typically dark wood strips over light stucco, usually on the upper floor or gable — [Dallas Tudor guide (search summary)](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/pnv/PublishingImages/Pages/Guides/Tudor_Style%20Details.pdf). Modern luxury TU plans use brick, stone and stucco — [Architectural Designs 9366EL](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/a-touch-of-tudor-9366el); [ThePlanCollection 33577](https://theplancollection.com/house-plans/plan-6179-square-feet-4-bedroom-5-5-bathroom-tudor-style-33577)
- QA walls: patterned shingles, half-timbering, patterned masonry, pebble panels, spindlework — [PHMC Queen Anne](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/queen-anne.html)

## 5. Garage handling in modern upscale builds

### Takeaway
In every luxury plan found, the garage is attached and side-entry: its doors face sideways, so the street front stays classical. In capes it often sits beyond a breezeway, and some plans use a detached garage linked by a breezeway, which reads as a carriage house.

### Cited Findings
- GE 750032LDG: attached three-car side-entry garage, 690 sq ft (64 m2) — [Architectural Designs 750032LDG](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/elegant-georgian-house-plan-featuring-five-bedrooms-and-three-car-garage-750032ldg). 72661DA and 196-1273 also have three-car side-entry garages; 720028DA has an angled garage; 32569 has a porte-cochère — search snippets: [Architectural Designs 720028DA](https://architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/5-bed-southern-georgian-house-plan-with-angled-garage-and-second-floor-sunset-balcony-720028da); [ThePlanCollection 32569](https://www.theplancollection.com/house-plans/plan-4448-square-feet-5-bedroom-5-5-bathroom-georgian-style-32569)
- TU: Rolling Green and Glen Hollow both have attached three-car side-entry garages — [Houseplansandmore 031D-0037](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan031D-0037.aspx); [Houseplansandmore 031D-0036](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan031D-0036.aspx)
- CC: side-entry attached garages (Shamrock Point, Edison Hill); a breezeway linking to a detached garage (Plan 174-1075); a 12' x 12' (3.7 x 3.7 m) breezeway in a resale listing — [Houseplansandmore 036D-0120](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan036D-0120.aspx); [ThePlanCollection 11291](https://theplancollection.com/house-plans/home-plan-11291); [Redfin listing](https://redfin.com/NH/Hill/41-Ferrin-St-03243/home/88181096)
- Old House Guy says garage openings should never get shutters — [Old House Guy](https://www.oldhouseguy.com/shutters-good-vs-bad-examples/)

### Inferences
- Generator rules by style:
  - GE and CR: a 1-1.5-storey garage wing about 10 x 7 m (three cars), using the main house's wall material and roof pitch, ridge 2-3 m lower, set back 1-2 m. Doors face the side yard; the street face of the wing gets 2 windows or blind arches.
  - CC: house, then a breezeway 3.7 m long, then a garage with dormers in the roof (a bonus room).
  - TU: a garage wing with steep gables and half-timbering in its gable, doors facing sideways; wood-look plank doors with small top lights.
  - QA: a detached carriage house at the rear (a small box with a gable or hip roof, a cupola, and the house's paint scheme).
- Side-load needs a turning court about 7-8 m in front of the doors, which takes lot depth; a 28 m lot can just fit it.

### Gaps

## 6. Signature details that read at game scale (and what to skip)

### Takeaway
Use details that show up in silhouette or as large-scale flat contrast: GE quoins, a heavy cornice band, a portico pediment; CC dormers and the chimney; dark TU timber strips on light stucco, steep gables and the big chimney; the QA tower cone, wraparound porch, and paint banding with fish-scale shingle texture. Leave out fine mouldings, spindles and carved brackets as geometry; use texture or skip them.

### Cited Findings
- GE: dentiled cornice, water table or belt course, decorative quoins, pediment over the door with pilasters — [PHMC Georgian](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/georgian.html)
- CR: broken pediments, fanlights, sidelights, porticos — [Wikipedia, Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Revival_architecture)
- Colonial trim hierarchy: horizontal trim (skirtboard, head casings, frieze) tall and robust, vertical trim narrower; wider corner boards; taller head casings; half-round banding — [Fine Homebuilding](https://finehomebuilding.com/2016/05/16/upgrading-colonial-exteriors)
- TU: half-timbering, steep cross gables, prominent chimneys, gabled entry, patterned brick or stone — [PHMC Tudor Revival](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/tudor-revival.html)
- QA: tower with conical roof, wraparound porch, multi-colour paint, patterned shingles and spindlework — [PHMC Queen Anne](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/architecture/Styles/queen-anne.html); [This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/painting/paint-color-ideas-for-ornate-victorian-houses)

### Inferences
- Keep as geometry (cheap boxes or prisms):
  - GE and CR: a cornice band (box 0.3 m high, projecting 0.25 m); corner quoins as alternating 0.3/0.2 m blocks stacked up each corner (GE only); a water table ledge at about 0.6 m; portico columns as 8-12-sided prisms with a box capital; a triangular pediment over the door and dormers; shutters as thin boxes 1-2 cm proud of the casing. This detail is valued on its own: depth casts the shadows that make it read as real.
  - CC: dormers as boxes with a triangular cap, and a chunky chimney.
  - TU: half-timber strips as thin raised boxes about 0.15-0.2 m wide, in patterns of verticals at 0.6 m centres, one horizontal at mid-height and short diagonal braces at the corners; a slight jetty (upper storey overhanging 0.2-0.3 m); a stepped chimney; a stone arch at the entry (a box with a half-disc cut-out or a texture).
  - QA: a tower cylinder (8-12 sides) and cone roof; a porch as a slab, posts, a roof and a simple rail; a gable rake shown by a contrasting trim plank; a bay window as a 3-sided prism.
- Do as texture only: dentils (a repeating small-block normal or albedo strip under the cornice), fish-scale shingles in QA gables and the upper storey, brick bond, diamond-pane leaded glass (TU), stucco grain, clapboard lines, 6-over-6 muntins.
- Skip: spindlework, gingerbread brackets, turned balusters (use a flat rail with simple square balusters or a solid panel), carved bargeboards, Corinthian capitals, fanlight tracery (use a half-disc of light glass), modillions, curved thatch-imitating eaves (TU), Flemish curved gables.

## 7. Price-signalling cues: expensive vs builder-grade

### Takeaway
What signals "expensive" is consistent across sources: correct proportions and depth (thick trim, shadow lines, real-looking shutters), real masonry and multiple materials, symmetry done exactly, a strong entrance, and garages hidden from the street. Builder-grade shows as flat vinyl, thin trim, mismatched or fixed-flat shutters, plastic grilles, and garage doors facing the street.

### Cited Findings
- Upgrading a basic colonial trim package (taller skirtboard with drip, wider corner boards, taller head casings with crown, entablatures over windows and entry, cornice returns, divided-lite sash, sidelites, robust pilasters, properly hung shutters, front porch) costs about 2% of construction — [Fine Homebuilding](https://finehomebuilding.com/2016/05/16/upgrading-colonial-exteriors)
- Cheap cues: plastic or vinyl shutters, shutters fixed flat to siding, mismatched shutter sizes, faux wood-grain siding, plastic window grilles. Authentic cues: operable-looking louvers, casing-mounted hinges, visible shadow depth, shutters shaped to the window, period-matching trim — [Old House Guy](https://www.oldhouseguy.com/shutters-good-vs-bad-examples/)
- Luxury plan markers: two-storey foyer, multiple gables and dormers, arched windows, a prominent covered entry, side-entry three-car garage, brick front — [Architectural Designs 750032LDG](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/elegant-georgian-house-plan-featuring-five-bedrooms-and-three-car-garage-750032ldg)
- Luxury TU plans emphasise detailed brick and stonework, and combinations of brick, stone and stucco — [Houseplansandmore 031D-0037](https://houseplansandmore.com/homeplans/houseplan031D-0037.aspx); [Architectural Designs 9366EL](https://www.architecturaldesigns.com/house-plans/a-touch-of-tudor-9366el)
- CR exaggerates the details and scale of its prototypes — [Wikipedia, Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Revival_architecture)

### Inferences
- A generator "luxury" switch could toggle:
  1. Trim depth: projecting cornice and casings vs flush.
  2. Real-material textures: brick or stone vs lap siding on the front.
  3. Roof material: slate or cedar-shake texture with visible thickness vs flat asphalt colour.
  4. Taller ceilings: about 3.0-3.3 m floor-to-floor instead of 2.7 m, plus taller windows.
  5. Chimneys: 2 or more masonry chimneys rising well above the ridge.
  6. Entrance: a pediment or portico, sidelights and fanlight.
  7. Garage: side-load or detached, never facing the street.
  8. Shutters: sized and mounted correctly, or none (never undersized).
  9. Roofline: a single coherent roof, not a jumble of builder gables, except for TU and QA, where controlled complexity is the style.
  10. Landscaping cues around the house (hedges, front walk on axis), which may sit outside the house generator.
- "McMansion" anti-cues to avoid: many unrelated gables, a two-storey entry box stuck on the front, a front-facing garage that dominates the facade, and different materials on the front only. This is common critique; I did not source it this session.
